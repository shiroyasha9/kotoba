# How kotoba's numbers were measured

The README compares kotoba with Wispr Flow. This page gives the method, the source and the limits of each number.

- **Memory.** Release build with Sparkle, idle 35 s after launch, no window opened: physical footprint from `vmmap --summary`, median of 5 launches (range 15.6-16.8 MB), on an M3 Pro with macOS 27. This was measured on the 0.1.0 shape of the app; see [`docs/research/memory-spike.md`](research/memory-spike.md). After use the number is higher. Release 0.4.1 after 25 and 100 minutes of the owner's use (dictations, the window opened and closed): 68 MB, peak 181 MB. Most of that was memory the window had freed but the allocator kept; from the build after 0.4.1 kotoba runs the allocator in its space-efficient mode, and `make window-memory` (the Debug build, offscreen, every pane shown and closed three times) then leaves 41 MB (median of 5, range 41-52 MB) where it left 69 MB. What stays is SwiftUI's loaded runtime; see [`docs/research/memory-spike.md`](research/memory-spike.md), "Memory after use". On release 0.4.2 after the owner's use, Activity Monitor showed 34.7 MB (2026-10-17). On release 0.4.6, 22 h 50 min after launch with its window closed, `footprint` read 73-75 MB (2026-10-21), 53 MB of it in Malloc Small; the README says "about 75 MB" and the site "about 80 MB", rounded up. The Wispr Flow figure is not ours: it is from [tl;dv's review](https://tldv.io/blog/wispr-flow-notetaker-review/) (Wispr Flow 1.6.937 at rest on an 8 GB iMac), quoted in [`docs/research/beyond-wispr.md`](research/beyond-wispr.md).
- **Accuracy.** 62 real dictations from one developer, mostly coding prompts in a terminal, each with a hand-corrected reference. kotoba ran MAI-Transcribe-2 through OpenRouter with the personal dictionary sent as a phrase list. The Wispr Flow column is Wispr's own speech recognition on the same clips (its raw transcript, before its formatter). The 95% confidence interval for kotoba's word error rate is 1.6-5.8%; 62 clips is a small set. Details in [`docs/research/quality-perf-2.md`](research/quality-perf-2.md); the harness is in [`bench/`](../bench/). The audio is personal, so the clips and the full report are not in this repository.
- **Round trip.** From the start of the upload to the full answer, for MAI-Transcribe-2 through OpenRouter with zero data retention on. 40 clips of 10-20 s, sent one at a time from a home network in India by the bench harness, not by the app. A second run without zero data retention gave p50 881 ms and p90 1,461 ms. The app's own target from stop tap to paste is p50 at most 1.0 s and p90 at most 1.5 s; `make latency-log` reports it from the app's unified log.
- **Stop tap to paste.** Release 0.4.6 in the owner's daily use on 2026-10-21, from the app's unified log (`make latency-log`): 11 pasted dictations of 10-20 s with MAI-Transcribe-2 through OpenRouter, p50 987 ms and p90 2,047 ms; all 36 dictations of that day, p50 951 ms and p90 2,005 ms. The median meets the 1.0 s target and the p90 does not meet the 1.5 s target: almost all of the time is the speech service's answer (p50 958 ms within a minute of the last dictation, p90 2,133 ms after a quiet minute). 11 dictations is a small set.
- **Price.** The speech requests of that bench cost $0.17 for 99.2 minutes of audio, which matches OpenRouter's list price of $0.10 per hour. MAI-Transcribe-2 is in public preview, and this price is a promotion until 2026-12-31. Cleanup, which is optional, costs extra. Wispr Flow's price is from its Pro plan ($15 per month, or $12 per month billed yearly), as quoted in `docs/research/beyond-wispr.md`.
- **Download.** The size of `kotoba-0.5.2.dmg` on the Releases page (7,039,816 bytes).

Two more numbers from the capture path (built-in mic, from the commit that moved capture to AUHAL): recording starts 38-47 ms after the key press, and after the stop tap kotoba waits at most 13 ms for the last audio, so your last word is kept.
